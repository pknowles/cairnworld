# The Stolen Flour

A minimal Cairn adventure for one Warden and one player.

**Expected play time:** 10–20 minutes
**Location:** One hut
**NPCs at the site:** One
**Immediate objective:** Recover as much of the village flour as possible

The adventure may end peacefully without a save. It may also lead to stealth, pursuit within the hut, negotiation, restraint or combat.

---

## Character Creation

Create a character normally, including their Background, Bond and equipment.

During the opening conversation, briefly connect the PC to Mara, the storehouse keeper. Choose or improvise one:

* Mara previously helped the PC.
* The PC’s Bond involves Mara or her household.
* The PC depends upon the village storehouse.
* The PC has a professional or personal reason to mediate disputes.
* The PC simply accepts responsibility for helping the settlement.

Do not offer a cash reward.

For immediate play, pregenerated characters may be provided separately.

---

## The Situation

Toma, an injured former farmhand, stole a sack of flour from the village storehouse. He has taken shelter in an abandoned charcoal-burner’s hut near the forest.

He is hungry, unable to find work and afraid of the village constable.

Mara asks the PC to recover the flour.

She says:

> “Toma took one of the large sacks and ran to the old charcoal hut. We need back whatever remains. Please don’t kill him over it.”

Mara knows the following if asked:

* Toma injured his leg several months ago.
* He has struggled to work since then.
* He has recently begged for food.
* The constable has threatened to imprison him for theft.
* The sack held enough flour to feed several households.
* Mara is angry, but does not want Toma badly hurt.

The hut is nearby. Nothing interrupts the journey.

---

# The Hut

## Exterior

The hut is a single low room made from warped boards.

It has two entrances:

* A front door, barred from inside.
* A rear window, open and wide enough to crawl through.

The hut stands slightly above the ground on old stone footings. Beneath the rear wall is a narrow crawlspace.

A careful look around the rear reveals:

* A loose floorboard above the crawlspace.
* Torn waxed cloth caught beneath it.
* Mouse-chewed crumbs.
* Faint old markings used by charcoal burners to identify an emergency cache.

Do not require a roll to notice these signs when the PC examines the area.

---

## Interior

Through cracks and the rear window, the PC can see:

* Toma sitting beside a weak fire.
* An open sack of flour beside him.
* Flour scattered across the floor.
* A piece of crude flatbread cooking on a stone.
* A wood axe within Toma’s reach.
* The wooden bar across the front door.
* Toma’s swollen and poorly wrapped leg.

Toma has not initially noticed the PC unless their approach was conspicuous.

---

## The Cache

Beneath the loose floorboard is an old emergency cache containing:

* Two uses of dried food.
* A small waterskin.
* A serviceable wool cloak.
* A handful of low-value copper coins.
* A rusted charcoal-burner’s hook.

The cache helps Toma, but does not completely solve his problem.

The wool cloak and food would make travel possible. The hook can lift the bar on the front door through a suitable gap or pull the cache closer.

### Reaching it

A PC may reach into the crawlspace from outside, but doing so places them directly beneath the rear window and occupies both hands.

If Toma is unaware or distracted, taking the cache may require no save.

If Toma is alert and watching the rear of the hut, reaching it unseen is risky. Explain the likely consequence before calling for a save.

Possible consequences include:

* Toma notices and claims the cache himself.
* He moves the flour toward the front door.
* He reaches the rear window before the PC can withdraw.
* He realizes the PC has been moving around the hut and becomes distrustful.

Suitable tools, unusual equipment, patience or a distraction may eliminate the risk.

---

# Toma

**5 HP, 10 STR, 12 DEX, 8 WIL**
**Wood axe, d6**

Toma has no Armor.

His leg is injured. He can walk, stand and fight, but:

* He cannot move quickly while carrying the flour.
* Running over rough ground would risk worsening the injury.
* Climbing through the window with the sack is impractical.
* Treatment or support would greatly improve his chances of leaving safely.

Do not apply a constant numerical penalty. Use the injury when it materially affects an action.

---

## Toma’s priorities

In order, Toma wants to:

1. Avoid imprisonment.
2. Obtain enough food and protection to reach another settlement.
3. Avoid further injury.
4. Keep as much flour as he can.

He values freedom and survival more than the flour.

---

## Toma’s beliefs

* The constable will imprison him.
* Anyone sent by Mara may intend to capture him.
* A verbal promise alone is unreliable.
* He cannot travel far without food, warmth and help for his leg.
* The PC might be dangerous.
* Killing the PC would bring worse consequences.

---

## Toma’s behavior

Toma will:

* Speak before attacking.
* Grab the axe when alarmed.
* Attempt to keep one exit available.
* Abandon the flour if it is the only way to escape.
* Accept a concrete arrangement that addresses both survival and freedom.
* Defend himself if cornered or attacked.
* Avoid killing a helpless PC.
* Stop trusting the PC if clearly deceived.

He will not:

* Exchange the flour for a predetermined price.
* Believe an unsupported promise merely because a save succeeds.
* Fight to the death for the sack.
* Pursue a PC who withdraws.

---

# Beginning the Encounter

After describing what the PC can see, ask:

> What do you do?

Do not provide a menu of approaches.

When Toma notices the PC, he grabs the axe and says:

> “Stay back. I only need enough to get away. I’m not going back to that cell.”

He does not attack immediately.

---

# Resolving Approaches

## Negotiation

Judge offers according to Toma’s priorities.

An offer is strong when it gives him some combination of:

* Food for the journey.
* Warm clothing.
* Treatment or support for his leg.
* A safe route away.
* Protection from the constable.
* Credible work or shelter.
* Space to leave before the PC takes the flour.

The cache provides several of these things, but the PC may still need to contribute something, offer treatment, establish trust or accept that Toma takes some flour.

A concrete and believable arrangement may simply work.

A plausible but uncertain appeal, bluff or threat may involve a save when failure has a meaningful consequence.

An impossible offer does not become believable because of a successful roll.

### Trust during an exchange

Toma does not willingly surrender the flour while standing within the PC’s reach.

A peaceful exchange may involve physical arrangements such as:

* Toma places the flour by one exit and withdraws through the other.
* The PC leaves supplies outside and backs away.
* The PC treats Toma before either side moves the flour.
* Both parties move simultaneously.
* The PC permits Toma to take a smaller quantity of flour.

Use these negotiations to make positioning relevant without requiring a formal subsystem.

---

## The cache

The cache can be:

* Given to Toma.
* Used to improve an offer.
* Taken by the PC.
* Used as bait.
* Retrieved secretly.
* Revealed during negotiation.
* Claimed by Toma if he reaches it first.

It is an opportunity, not a mandatory solution.

---

## The front door

The door is barred from inside.

The PC may:

* Convince Toma to open it.
* Lift the bar using the charcoal hook or another tool.
* Manipulate it through gaps in the boards.
* Break the door.
* Enter through the window instead.

A sensible, unopposed method works without a save.

Forcing the door while Toma opposes the PC is risky. A failure must change the situation, such as allowing Toma to reach the flour, the cache or the opposite exit.

---

## The rear window

The window permits easy entry when no one resists.

Entering while Toma is alert and positioned to stop the PC is dangerous. The likely consequence might be:

* Being pushed back outside.
* Becoming exposed to the axe.
* Allowing Toma to escape through the door.
* Knocking over the flour or fire.

State the likely consequence before resolving the attempt.

---

## The flour

The sack is bulky, open and close to the fire.

It is not automatically damaged by ordinary handling. It is endangered by actions such as:

* Wrestling over it.
* Dragging it rapidly.
* Overturning the fire.
* Bursting violently through the door.
* Swinging weapons recklessly beside it.
* Using water or smoke without protecting it.

Warn the player when an action clearly threatens the flour.

Possible results are:

* Nearly all flour recovered.
* Some flour spilled or consumed.
* The sack abandoned by Toma.
* Most of the flour ruined.
* Toma escaping with part of it.

Avoid measuring exact weights unless the player’s plan requires it.

---

## Toma’s injury

The injury can become leverage, common ground or an obstacle.

Possible uses include:

* Offering treatment.
* Making a walking support.
* Helping Toma across rough ground.
* Recognizing that he cannot carry the sack far.
* Threatening the injury.
* Exploiting it while restraining him.
* Using it to demonstrate that flight without help is unrealistic.

How the PC treats the injury should affect Toma’s attitude.

Medical assistance does not automatically solve the dispute. It makes cooperation and escape more plausible.

---

## Equipment and Backgrounds

Let specific equipment change what is possible.

Examples:

* A climbing aid permits a silent approach from an unexpected direction.
* A hook or long tool manipulates the barred door.
* Restraints prevent escape but make peaceful trust unlikely.
* Medical equipment makes treatment credible.
* Agricultural experience reveals how much flour is usable.
* Criminal experience identifies the cache marks and crude security.
* A cloak, ration or waterskin can become part of a bargain.
* A strange item may create an entirely unexpected solution.

An item may remove one obstacle completely. It should not automatically decide what happens to Toma, the flour and the village.

---

# Escalation

Escalate only in response to the PC’s actions or a failed risky action.

Useful escalations include:

1. Toma becomes aware of the PC.
2. Toma takes the axe.
3. Toma moves toward an exit.
4. Toma claims the cache.
5. The flour is placed at risk.
6. An exit becomes blocked.
7. Trust breaks down.
8. Someone attacks.

Do not advance this sequence automatically. A careful or compassionate player may prevent escalation entirely.

---

# Violence

Toma fights only when attacked, cornered or convinced that capture is unavoidable.

His goals during violence are:

* Create space.
* Reach an exit.
* Make the PC release him.
* Abandon the sack if necessary.
* Survive.

He may overturn furniture, threaten the flour, shove past the PC or use the axe defensively.

When his courage breaks, he flees or surrenders according to which appears safer.

Toma does not kill a helpless PC. If he incapacitates the character and can escape, he takes useful food or clothing, leaves the heavy flour and departs.

---

# Endings

End the adventure once the situation at the hut is resolved.

## Peaceful exchange

Toma receives enough practical help to leave and relinquishes most or all of the flour.

## Compromise

Toma takes supplies, some flour or assistance. The PC returns the remainder.

## Reconciliation

The PC creates a credible arrangement under which Toma can return without immediate imprisonment.

## Recovery by cunning

The PC obtains the flour through positioning, distraction, equipment or stealth. Toma escapes or is left behind.

## Capture

The PC restrains Toma and returns him with the flour.

## Mercy

The PC lets Toma leave with part or all of the flour.

## Violence

Toma is injured or killed and some or all of the flour is recovered.

## Failure

Toma escapes with the flour, or the flour is destroyed.

None is designated as the correct ending.

---

# Returning to Mara

Mara’s response should reflect what happened.

* She is relieved by the return of most of the flour.
* She accepts a modest loss if bloodshed was avoided.
* She is upset if Toma was killed unnecessarily.
* She objects if all the flour was surrendered, but listens to the PC’s reasoning.
* She will consider helping Toma if the PC returns with a credible arrangement.
* She cannot personally guarantee what the constable will do.

Do not turn this into a second encounter. Resolve the immediate reaction briefly and end.

---

# Warden Guidance

## Keep the central tension visible

The encounter is about three incompatible claims:

* The village needs the flour.
* Toma needs the means to survive.
* Toma fears losing his freedom.

Every ruling should follow from those facts.

## Do not protect intended solutions

The cache may be ignored.

Toma may be captured immediately.

The PC may surrender all the flour.

An unusual item may bypass an obstacle.

The adventure does not depend on a particular sequence.

## Distinguish information from risk

Give visible and reasonably inferable information freely.

Use uncertainty only where an action exposes someone or something to a meaningful consequence.

Do not make finding the content of the adventure itself the challenge.

## Make failures alter position

A failed risky action should not invite an identical retry.

Move Toma, end an opportunity, threaten the flour, change his trust, expose the PC or transfer control of an exit or resource.

## Let character creation matter

A Background should change how the PC understands or approaches the hut.

Their inventory should create genuine opportunities.

Do not rebalance the scene to nullify a useful character ability.

## Avoid forcing mechanics

Do not manufacture combat, saves or damage merely to test them.

The adventure succeeds when the player understands the situation and makes consequential choices.
